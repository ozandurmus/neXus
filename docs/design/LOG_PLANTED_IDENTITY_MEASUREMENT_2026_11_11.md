# Planted-identity test (design measurement E5) - 2026-11-11

Status: EVIDENCE RECORD (non-authoritative). It decides nothing and approves no code, command or outbound connection.
It closes the part of design measurement E5 that
[LOG_MASKING_RISK_MEASUREMENT_2026_11_10.md](LOG_MASKING_RISK_MEASUREMENT_2026_11_10.md) left open (§1 and §10 "Not
changed: the planted-identity corpus"), for the DRAFT design
[LOG_ANOMALY_AGENT_DESIGN_2026_11_10.md](LOG_ANOMALY_AGENT_DESIGN_2026_11_10.md) (§3.2 masked classes, §3.3 egress
profile, §3.5 egress guard, §10 E5) and the PO decision record
[PO_DECISION_RECORD_2026_11_10_LOG_ANOMALY_AGENT.md](PO_DECISION_RECORD_2026_11_10_LOG_ANOMALY_AGENT.md).

Synthetic data only. The estate, the names, the addresses, the serials and the log lines were generated by the
program from a fixed seed. Nothing was read from HOST-A, a device, a database or a log pool, and no model was called.

## 0. Answer in short

- **Known identities are masked.** Of 4,180 planted values that the dictionary holds (names of eight classes, device
  names, serials and addresses, in 23 contexts), 4,158 are gone after the masking. The 22 that survive are two
  specific cases, both about letter case (findings 2 and 3). Every shape was caught: names with spaces, hyphens, dots,
  `/` and `+`, names that contain an address, cluster members, all four address ranges.
- **Unknown identities are not.** A name that is not in the dictionary survives the masking in 56 % of the planted
  cases (1,955 of 3,480). The masking protects the contexts that carry a trigger word (`user=`, `account "..."`,
  `login by`, `for ... from`, the JSON `admin` key, the syslog header host) and none of the others. This is the case
  the design must survive: a new administrator, a deleted object, a name that only the logs know.
- **The drafted egress profile removes them.** With the technical vocabulary only (p3), **0 of 3,480** unknown names
  survive, across three seeds. With the English word list of the draft (p2), 140 survive (4.0 %): names that are
  ordinary English words. This confirms Argon finding 1 with a count: the English word list must not be used.
- **One class of identity cannot be removed by any vocabulary filter: a name that is itself a vocabulary word.**
  970 of 1,400 planted vocabulary-word names (69 %) are released, all of them in contexts without a trigger word. The
  dictionary does not hold them either (the catalogue drops generic names on purpose). This test measures the
  mechanism, not the rate: on the real lines of the risk measurement the profile left 21 lines in 2.9 million.
- **One real gap in the profile: hex-encoded addresses.** A value such as `0x` plus eight hex digits is kept by the
  profile's hex-literal allowance, and the guard has no shape for it. 55 of 100 planted survive. Excluding vocabulary-
  word names, secrets and this encoding, **0 of 9,860 planted values survive p3**; with it, 55 of 9,960.
- **The guard is a backstop, and it works as one.** While the masking and the profile work, the guard never fired on
  an identity (G2 and G3: 0 of 11,010 planted lines; G4 blocked the 35 private-key-header lines). It blocked 0 of 228
  control lines. When the masking is switched off in the test (raw lines into the profile and the guard), the guard
  blocks all 330 known names the profile left, all 640 addresses and serials, and 405 of 470 network shapes; it
  releases the 65 hex-encoded addresses, 10 unknown names and 1,260 of 1,330 vocabulary-word names. It adds almost nothing for unknown names
  (13 of 2,475 survivors blocked): it cannot know what the dictionary does not hold.
- **Two small masking findings** (§4): the estate-name digest folds case with the root locale, so a known name with a
  Turkish `i`, dotless `i` or dotted capital `I` in a different case is not masked (12 of 20 planted); and device and
  host names are matched exactly by design, so a plain name in the other case is not masked (10 of 10 planted). The
  profile removes both. Neither is a leak at p3.
- **Limits.** Invented pronounceable words, 23 hand-written contexts, a model of the profile and of the guard, an
  approximated alias step, no real estate. The numbers prove that the mechanisms work on the planted shapes; they do
  not prove that no real shape is missed (§5).

## 1. What was asked

The PO session asked (2026-11-11, "sahte kimlik testine bakalim"): plant synthetic identities - names, addresses,
serials, administrator, policy, object, zone and virtual-system names shaped like the estate's - into a synthetic
corpus, push it through `logmask-v5` plus the drafted egress profile and guard, and report the catch rate per class.
The risk measurement had counted what survives on real stored lines; it could not say what happens to an identity
that is *not* in the dictionary, nor whether the profile and the guard hold when the masking misses.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 What ran

- `scripts/measurement/log_planted_identity/PlantedIdentityMeasure.java`, compiled against the deployed service jar
  (the classes of the running image, built 2026-11-10 17:42, which contain `logmask-v5` and `EstateNameCatalog`) and run
  on the workstation. It needs no live estate dictionary, so nothing ran in the pod.
- The **synthetic estate** is loaded through the product's own classes: administrator, policy, rule, object, zone,
  virtual-system, domain and host names through `EstateNameCatalog` (a `Source` that returns the synthetic names), device
  names, serials and addresses through `EstateDictionarySource` and `KnownNames`, with the `TopologyNamePseudonymizer`
  pseudonyms. The catalogue's own filters apply (generic words, address-like values, splitting at other characters).
- The **corpus** plants one identity (two in the `multi` context) per line. A line is a Check Point syslog line, an
  elg line or a Palo Alto JSON line of the measured shapes (risk record §2 and findings record of 2026-10-17). The
  plant sits in one of 22 contexts: after `user=`, in quotes, after `for`, in brackets, after a separator, after a
  non-vocabulary word, in a sentence, before punctuation, glued to text with `_` or `-`, in a path, in a URL path,
  possessive, upper case (root and Turkish locale, with and without a trigger word), as a JSON value, in free JSON text,
  in an elg line, as the header host, and twice on one line.
- Every identity class has several **shapes** (70 class and shape combinations; 118 with known and unknown): administrator names (plain,
  English word, `first.last`, service account, name plus digits, Turkish letters), policy names (underscores with a year,
  hyphen, several words, English phrase, Turkish letters), rule, object (name containing an address, host-like, group,
  `A/B`, `A+B`, plain), zone, virtual system, domain, host (plain label, label plus digits, FQDN), inventory device names
  (site-role-number, cluster member, plain), serials (Palo Alto digits, Check Point alphanumeric, hex-like), management
  addresses (four ranges), and network shapes (CIDR, IPv6 global, ULA and mapped, MAC in three forms, e-mail, public and
  internal FQDN, URL host, and five encodings of an address: dashes, underscores, reverse-DNS, hex, decimal).
  Secret-shaped values (password, SNMP community, PSK, bearer token, hex key, private-key header) are built at run time
  from parts.
- Each class and shape is planted twice: **known** (its values are in the dictionary) and **unknown** (the dictionary
  does not hold them: a name that exists only in logs, a deleted object, an administrator created after the last
  refresh). Known and unknown pools share no value.
- A third group, **vocabulary-word names**, plants identities whose value is itself a technical-vocabulary word (a zone
  or an administrator called by an ordinary word of the vocabulary). Of 18 candidate words, 10 are in the 1,780-word
  vocabulary of the risk measurement.
- 11,238 lines: 11,010 carry planted values (11,570 values in all) and 228 are control lines (the same contexts with a
  neutral vocabulary word and no identity). Three seeds, which change every synthetic value and keep the shapes.

### 2.2 The stages

| Stage | What it is |
|---|---|
| p0 | `LogMasking` `logmask-v5` on the line, one fresh session per line (no learning from earlier lines), as deployed |
| p2 | the egress profile as drafted in design §3.3: every word not in the technical vocabulary or the English word list becomes `<W>`; numbers up to five digits stay (the workstation's 234,428-word list) |
| p3 | the profile with Argon finding 1: technical vocabulary only, every number typed `<N>` except short codes after `signal`, `exit`, `errno`, `status`, `code`; a sub-interface number becomes `<VLAN>` |
| guard | a model of design §3.5 on the p3 text: G2 (a word equals an identity of the dictionary), G3 (an address-, MAC-, e-mail-, FQDN- or URL-shaped string, or an IPv6 address, that is not a substitution made by the masker and not in a kept range), G4 (a secret marker with a value, a private-key header, a long high-entropy run) |
| raw input | the same profile and guard on the line as if the masking had failed open (the header device name replaced, since it would block every line) |

The profile and the guard exist only in this program, as a model of the DRAFT design. No product code implements them.
G1 (rule-set pin), G5 (structure) and G6 (canary self-test) have nothing to test on a line and are not modelled. The
request-scoped aliases and relative dates of §3.3 are not applied. The alias step of §3.3 is modelled as the design
intends it: a token that the masker changed is an alias; a token it left alone is not. The program derives the changed
tokens by comparing the raw and the masked line (see finding 8).

A planted value **survives** a stage when it occurs in the stage's output as a whole word, in any letter case of either
locale. A planted value is **released** when it survives p3 and the guard did not block the line. Survival is stricter
than leakage for glued values (a name inside `cfg_name_backup` counts), and generous for the one case where the value
is only a piece of what the line shows.

## 4. Findings

1. **The dictionary catches every planted known identity except two case situations.** 4,158 of 4,180 (99.5 %) across
   eight name classes with 38 shapes, device names, serials and addresses. Names containing an address, names split at
   `/` or `+`, names of several words and cluster members are masked.
2. **Estate names and Turkish letters.** The estate-name digest lower-cases with the root locale
   (`EstateDictionary`, the digest of `value.toLowerCase(Locale.ROOT)`). The dotless `i` and the dotted capital `I` of
   Turkish do not round-trip through that: `I` lower-cases to `i`, not to `i` without a dot, and the Turkish capital of
   `i` lower-cases to `i` plus a combining dot. A known name written in the other case is therefore not matched. Planted
   known names with Turkish letters: stored case 0 of 140 survive, root-locale upper case 2 of 10, Turkish-locale upper
   case 10 of 10; after a trigger word 0 of 20 (the trigger rule masks it). Whether real logs carry such case
   variants is UNKNOWN. A fold that maps `i`, dotless `i` and dotted capital `I` to one letter on both sides of the
   digest would close it; the guard's G2 needs the same fold.
3. **Device and host names are exact by design.** `EstateDictionary` documents device identities as exact and
   case-sensitive (the identity law). A plain device or host name (no digit, no hyphen) in upper case is not masked at
   p0: 10 of 10 planted (5 device names, 5 host labels). Names with a digit or a hyphen are caught by the hostname
   shape rule in both cases. This is a consequence of a design choice, not a defect; the profile removes the name.
4. **Unknown names are the case that matters, and the vocabulary-only profile handles it.** 1,955 of 3,480 survive the
   masking; p3 leaves none. The English word list (p2) lets 140 through, so the draft's choice of word list is the wrong
   one (Argon finding 1).
5. **Vocabulary-word identities are a structural residual.** A name that is an ordinary technical word (and is therefore
   in the vocabulary, and dropped from the dictionary as generic) cannot be removed by a filter that keeps vocabulary
   words. 970 of 1,400 are released; all in contexts without a trigger word. Options for the design, for the PO session
   to weigh: accept the residual (it was 21 lines in 2.9 million on real data, all of them generic names); or keep
   generic-named estate names in a dictionary used only at egress, which masks `inside`-type names too and costs
   meaning; or exclude lines that contain such a word in a name position, which needs the policy and object context
   that a log line does not carry.
6. **Hex-encoded addresses pass the profile.** The profile keeps `0x` literals (they are error codes and pointers) and
   the guard has no shape for them: 55 of 100 planted survive and are released; the guard blocks 5 of 70 in the raw-
   input scenario. Restricting the allowance to short literals (four hex digits or fewer, say) removes the gap; the
   other encodings (dashes, underscores, reverse-DNS, decimal) end as words or `<N>` at p3. Under p2 the underscore
   form survives (75 of 100): p2 keeps numbers of up to five digits.
7. **The guard rarely fires in the working pipeline, and that is the right result.** It adds value as a backstop
   against a masking failure: in the raw-input scenario it blocks every known name the profile leaves and every address
   and serial; it does not help with unknown names, which is why the vocabulary profile carries that weight. The fail-
   closed rule of design §3.5 (a guard hit blocks the whole request) cost no false block on 228 control lines.
8. **G3 needs the masker's own substitution list.** A masked address is address-shaped, so a shape scan cannot tell it
   from a leaked one; the design resolves this by replacing the masker's substitutions with aliases first. The masker
   currently discards its substitutions (`TokenSink.DISCARD`). This test derived them by comparing the raw and the
   masked line, which works while the raw line is at hand (it is, when the egress payload is built) and which fails
   if a substitution changes token boundaries. The implementation needs a recorded substitution list or the same
   comparison, and a test that a leaked address next to a masked one is still seen.
9. **Secrets are not the masking's job and the profile covers them.** Password and community values survive the masking
   (the stored text is redacted at write time; the redactor is not part of this test) and are removed by p3 as non-
   vocabulary words. Keys of hex or mixed form are already masked at p0 by the serial-like rule. The private-key header
   is blocked by G4 (35 of 35).
10. **Profile cost.** 10.3 % of the word tokens of the control lines are replaced. See §3.8 for the real-text figure
    from the risk measurement.

## 5. Limits

- **Invented words.** The names are pronounceable syllable strings and a short list of English words. Real names are
  abbreviations, acronyms, Turkish words, company and product names, names with mixed scripts. The English list does
  not cover Turkish words (it would not have protected them either). The shapes were taken from the risk
  measurement's descriptions; a shape that was not planted is not measured.
- **Hand-written contexts.** 23 templates built from the measured line forms. Real lines have contexts that nobody
  listed; the real-data run of the risk measurement covers those, in the other direction.
- **Models, not product code.** The profile and the guard are this program's reading of the DRAFT design. The real ones
  will differ. G1, G5 and G6 are not modelled; request aliases and relative dates are not applied; the alias step is a
  token comparison.
- **One session per line.** Production masks a page (64 KiB) in one session, where a host name learned after a keyword
  carries to later lines. This test is the stricter case.
- **Secrets.** The write-time redactor is in the worker module and not on the classpath of the service jar; secret
  values reach the masking unredacted here.
- **No live dictionary.** Scale (20,000 object names), refresh behaviour and the first-load fail-closed path are not
  tested. The risk measurement ran the same masking with the live dictionary on 2.9 million real lines.
- **Case and locale.** Only the root and the Turkish locale were tried; other scripts (Cyrillic, Greek final sigma,
  German sharp s) were not.
- **The vocabulary** is the 1,780-word stand-in of the risk measurement, which `security_admin` is meant to review before
  an implementation.
- **The numbers are shape coverage, not probabilities.** 99.5 % of planted known identities masked says that the
  shapes planted are handled, not that 99.5 % of real identities are.

## 6. What this record changes in the DRAFT design

1. §3.3: the English word list goes (Argon finding 1, now with a count); the vocabulary is the only allow list.
2. §3.3: hex literals are kept only when short.
3. §3.3 and §3.5: state the vocabulary-word residual in the PO decision on Level 2 and decide between the three options
   of finding 5.
4. §3.5: G3 depends on a substitution list from the masker (finding 8); say so, and add a test where a leaked address
   sits next to a masked one.
5. §3.5: G2 must fold case the way the dictionary does, and the dictionary should fold Turkish `i` (finding 2); device
   names stay exact unless the PO session changes the identity-law reading (finding 3).
6. §10: E5 is done for the synthetic part. What remains before a Level 2 decision is a re-run of the risk measurement
   with the finished profile and guard on real lines, with zero surviving identities as the target.

## 7. How it was run (reproduce)

Source in `scripts/measurement/log_planted_identity/`: `PlantedIdentityMeasure.java`, `tables.py`. The technical

```sh
# the service jar of the running image (code only), unpacked

unzip -q service.jar 'BOOT-INF/lib/*' 'BOOT-INF/classes/*' -d lib

  java -Xmx2g -Dfile.encoding=UTF-8 -cp "out:lib/BOOT-INF/classes:lib/BOOT-INF/lib/*" \
    com.securityexpert.nexus.ui2.service.systemlog.PlantedIdentityMeasure \
    --tech techvocab.txt --english /usr/share/dict/words --seed $seed > run_$seed.tsv
done
python3 tables.py run_20261111.tsv run_7.tsv run_90210.tsv
```

A run takes about 3 seconds. The program prints fixed labels and numbers only (every printed field must match
`[a-z0-9_.]+`). `kubectl exec ... cat` of the 157 MB jar broke at 152 MB once; the rest was fetched with `tail -c +N`.
